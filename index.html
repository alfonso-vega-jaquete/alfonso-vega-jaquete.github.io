Selected work

  • Causal inference · Undergraduate thesis

    Spain's 22% minimum-wage increase had no detectable effect on youth participation

    A synthetic control built from 13 European countries puts the average effect at about −1 point, right in the middle of the placebo countries.

    R · gsynth · Eurostat panel, 2010 to 2022

  • Business impact · El Corte Inglés

    A sales decline blamed on one product was really about small orders

    Took small-order sales in a beverage category from −12% to +2%, about €1M, and measured about 15 CRM campaigns against holdout groups.

    SQL · Snowflake · Excel

  • Labor economics · Team project

    A gender pay gap that turned out to be a parenthood gap

    A 20.8% raw gap falls to about $5,800 with controls, and what remains sits with parents. Among childless employees it isn't statistically different from zero.

    R · regression with interactions · executive memo

  • Machine learning

    Ranking employees by attrition risk finds twice as many leavers as chance

    I caught a data leak that inflated the AUC to 0.95. The honest model reaches about half of all leavers by contacting a quarter of staff.

    Python · scikit-learn · random forest and neural network

About

I'm from Madrid and studied economics with a minor in data science at CUNEF, where my thesis used synthetic control to study Spain's 2019 minimum-wage increase. At El Corte Inglés I spent two years writing SQL against a six-million-customer database, testing CRM campaigns against holdout groups and presenting the results to category and purchasing leadership. Georgetown's MS in Business Analytics is the next step on a path that has run from economics to causal inference to data science. I'm especially drawn to competition and antitrust work, where the core question is usually what a market would have looked like otherwise.
