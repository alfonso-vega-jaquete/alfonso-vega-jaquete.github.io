A sales decline blamed on one product was really about small orders

A beverage category at El Corte Inglés was losing small-order sales, and the high-value product that had been flagged wasn't the reason. I traced the decline to small restocking orders and recommended targeted small-basket promotions, which took small-order sales from −12% to +2%, about €1M.

Problem

Small-order sales in a beverage category were down 12%, and the attention was on one high-value product that had been flagged as the cause.

Approach

I broke the category's sales down in Snowflake and found that the decline came from small restocking orders, not from the flagged product. The judgment call was following that breakdown instead of the explanation the business already had.

Result

I recommended targeted promotions for small baskets. Small-order sales went from −12% to +2%, worth about €1M.

Measuring what campaigns really added

The same question, what actually caused a change, ran through my CRM work. Across about 15 campaigns I compared customers who received an offer with matched holdout groups who didn't, so each campaign was measured against what would have happened anyway. The tests showed 15% more new customers than the holdouts, and comparing offers on incremental results rather than raw response pointed to the most cost-efficient design, which lifted retention by 4 points.

I also built the first automated dashboard for category leadership, with SQL in Snowflake feeding Excel. It was live in six categories when I left, with a roadmap to about 20, and replaced an estimated 10 hours of manual reporting per category.

Robustness and limits

The holdout groups are what make the campaign figures credible: they measure what the campaigns caused, not what happened at the same time. The beverage result is a before-and-after comparison, so part of the swing could come from the market rather than the promotions.

What I would do differently

Keep a holdout on the promotions too, so the beverage result would carry the same weight as the campaign tests.
